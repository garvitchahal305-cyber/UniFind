Security & Privacy Standard

Campus Safety & Guidelines

Your safety and privacy are paramount. Follow these official university recommendations to ensure secure handovers and avoid scams.

1. Use Designated Safe Exchange Zones

Always conduct exchanges in well-trafficked, well-lit public campus locations during daylight or operational hours.

Recommended Zones:
  • Central Library Reception / Security Desk
  • Student Union Hub Main Foyer
  • Campus Police & Security Station (Bldg 4, Rm 102)
  • Main Dining Hall Common Seating Area

Never agree to meet alone in isolated parking decks, off-campus alleys, or private dorm rooms with strangers.

2. Verify Genuine Ownership

Before handing over any valuable belonging, request indisputable proof of ownership from the claimant.

Proof of Ownership Methods:
  • Laptops / Phones: Require the claimant to unlock the lock-screen passcode in front of you.
  • Wallets / IDs: Ask for a government ID or photo comparison matching the student profile.
  • Bags / Backpacks: Ask them to describe specific unseen contents inside pockets.
  • Keys: Ask them to test the lock or key fob button on their vehicle/dorm.

3. Handling High-Value & Sensitive Items

Certain items require strict handling to protect student identity and financial security.

Immediate Security Surrender:
  • Credit & Debit Cards: Do not photograph or hold. Surrender immediately to Security so bank issuers can be notified.
  • Passports & Visas: Turn directly into the International Student Services or Campus Police.
  • Master Keys / Dorm Keys: Submit to Campus Housing to prevent unauthorized access.

4. Anti-Scam & Phishing Awareness

Stay vigilant against fraudulent requests or unauthorized fee solicitations.

Remember These Rules:
  • Zero Shipping Fees: UniFind is an on-campus platform. Never send money via wire, Venmo, or Gift Cards for "shipping fees" from another city.
  • No Password Inquiries: No campus staff member will ever ask for your University portal password.
  • Verified Emails: Always verify that emails come from a legitimate campus domain (`@university.edu`).
